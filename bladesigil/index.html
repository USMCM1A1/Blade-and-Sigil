How the Game Works

Novamagus, your home base

Every run starts in town. Walk into the inn for a safe night's rest (full HP and spell points, for a fee), the shop to buy gear and sell loot at half value, or the temple to bring fallen heroes back — for a steep offering. The dungeon gate leads below; the stairs at the bottom of the dungeon bring you home. Time stands still in town.

Exploring the endless dungeon

The party moves as one token. Walk into a door to open it, a chest to loot it (gold — often potions or gear), the stairs ▼ to descend or ▲ to climb back toward town. Every floor below the first is different each run, and they grow crueler with depth — but a floor you've cleared stays cleared until the run ends. Monsters that can see you give chase. Waiting and every step cost one turn.

Levels & the depths below

Slain monsters grant XP; reaching the next level costs 50 × your current level. When a hero has earned enough, a gold cross ✚ appears on their portrait — open the character sheet (C or I) and press Level up to take it: you roll your class hit die for new HP (a hero starts with the full die at level 1, and rolls of 1 are always rerolled), and a summary shows everything the level granted (to-hit, attacks, AC, spell points). The inventory screen always shows each hero's XP bar. When every hero stands at level 20, the next stairs down lead somewhere no map has charted. Bring everything you have.

Paths & powers

At level 5 some classes reach a fork: two paths, one choice, forever. Your path leans your numbers, grants an always-on knack, then a signature move at level 10 (a warrior of the Blade rampages from kill to kill; one of the Shield can take a blow meant for a friend — you'll be asked Y/N when it matters), a capstone at 15 that names you (Barbarian, Knight...) and joins the C menu, and a sharpening of your signature at 18. At level 20 comes the Rite: a power no other soul commands — you name it yourself — a personal sigil, and a title earned by how you actually fought, with rewards for the greatest deeds. A natural 20 is always a critical hit: it cannot miss, and deals the weapon's maximum plus a fresh damage roll.

Stealth & surprise

Start a fight on your terms — walk into a monster — and the enemy begins unaware (a 💤 marks them) until each takes its first turn or first wound. Thieves live on this: Vital Strike bites harder against the unaware and the flanked (a foe beside two heroes), and from level 10 an Assassinate against an unaware mark is an automatic critical. The Shadows path can Vanish mid-battle: monsters lose you utterly — you're a ghost on the field — until you strike from the dark. Monsters that catch you were ready all along: no surprise for the ambushed.

Traps & secret doors

Deeper floors hide traps in the floor and doors in the walls. Thieves and archers sense them as the party passes (their class tables + 5% per DEX modifier; wood elves are naturally sharp-eyed) — pressing Space searches again on purpose. A spotted trap shows ⚠: walk around it, or walk onto it to let your thief try to disarm it. A spotted secret door glows — walk into it to swing it open.

Battle

Bumping a monster (or being caught) pulls every nearby monster onto a tactical battlefield. Initiative (d20 + DEX for heroes) sets the order — the tracker at the top right shows it. A hero moves up to 4 squares; attacking, shooting, casting, or drinking a potion is their action and ends the turn. Front-row heroes start closest to the enemy. Slow monsters act only on even rounds. The fallen stay where they drop.

Magic & shooting

Spells come in levels 1–5: your character level sets how deep you can reach (level-2 spells at 4, level-3 at 8, level-4 at 12, level-5 at 16), and each cast costs spell points equal to its level ×2 +1 (3/5/7/9/11). Damage spells never miss — the target instead saves (d20 + save bonus vs DC 10 + spell level + caster's stat) for half damage; magic missile can't be resisted at all. Bursts like fireball burn friend and foe alike. The character sheet lists every spell you know. A forked Wizard follows one of two arts: a spellbook (broad — copies scrolls in, but only prepared spells are castable, re-picked at every rest) or the raw gift (narrow — a few spells chosen forever, each cast one point cheaper). Bows aim and damage with DEX and need both hands; light blades (finesse) swing with the better of STR or DEX. When you aim, the footer shows your odds.

Items & equipment

Loot goes into one shared pouch; gold is shared too. Each hero wears their own gear on the paper doll — head, necklace, armor, boots, two rings, and two hands (weapon, shield, or a two-hander that claims both). Classes have rules: priests swing blunt weapons, wizards wear no armor, only warriors and priests carry shields. Drinking a potion passes a turn. Heroes set out with rusty and splintered weapons that hit a little softer (−1 damage) — a clean blade from a shop or a chest is a real find.

Wounds, poison & camp

Poison keeps ticking as you explore — cure it with an Antidote or wait it out. Burning and paralysis last only through a battle. Camping (T, no enemies in sight) restores everyone's HP and spell points, but it isn't free: camp eats one ration per living hero (the shop sells them, chests hold them, and the party can only carry so many), and sometimes something finds the fire — you wake half-rested and fighting, the rations already eaten. A full watch passes either way — poison bites through the night, so cure first. The fallen stay fallen; greater magic than a nap will come later.

Running away

Fleeing works — but every enemy beside a hero lands a parting blow, and though the foes regroup for a few turns while you run, wounded monsters remember: if they catch you again, the rematch starts with them right on top of you.

The training arena

The backtick door leads to a sparring hall: one of every monster at 5× health, free spells and potions, no experience, no consequences. Everything is exactly as it was when you step back out.
